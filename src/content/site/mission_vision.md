# The Workshop

The Workshop is a shared place for **making, repairing, learning, and experimenting together**.

It is built around a simple idea: people learn useful skills by doing real work alongside other people.

This isn't a classroom where one person has all the answers, and it isn't a service where someone else does the work for you. It is a place to bring curiosity, projects, broken things, half-formed ideas, and skills worth sharing.

## Our Vision

We imagine a community where practical knowledge moves freely between generations.

A place where a teenager can learn electronics from someone who has worked with computers for decades—and then teach that person something they discovered yesterday.

Where tools are shared instead of sitting unused.

Where repairing something is an opportunity to understand how it works.

Where building a sensor, fixing a machine, writing some software, wiring a circuit, or constructing a wall becomes an opportunity to learn how to work with other people.

Over time, we hope the Workshop becomes part of the **shared infrastructure of our community**: a place where skills, tools, mentorship, experimentation, and relationships accumulate.

## Our Mission

**To create a collaborative workshop where people build practical skills, shared infrastructure, and community through hands-on participation.**

We do this by working on real things together.

We take things apart.

We repair what we can.

We build things we need.

We experiment.

We document what we learn.

We teach each other.

And when we don't know something, we figure it out together.

## Learning by Building

The Workshop starts with projects rather than curriculum.

A project might be:

- repairing an old piece of electronics
- building an ESP32 sensor
- learning to solder
- writing software
- building furniture
- understanding a circuit
- restoring an old computer
- creating tools for monitoring the river
- fixing something that would otherwise be thrown away
- building infrastructure needed here on the land

The project produces something useful, but **the object isn't the only product**.

The knowledge, confidence, relationships, and capacity created while building it matter just as much.

## Everyone Can Contribute

We don't divide people neatly into teachers and students.

Someone may know how to solder but not how to program.

Someone else may understand software but have never used a multimeter.

A teenager may recognize a technology an adult has never encountered. An experienced builder may know a trick that isn't written in any manual.

Everyone arrives knowing something.

Everyone arrives not knowing something.

That makes everyone capable of both learning and teaching.

## Young People Are Contributors

Young people aren't clients of the Workshop.

They are participants, contributors, apprentices, teachers, builders, and eventually stewards of the space itself.

The goal isn't simply to provide activities for young people. It is to create opportunities for them to participate in **meaningful work that actually matters**.

That means real tools, real problems, real responsibility, and real contributions—with appropriate support and attention to safety.

## Tools as a Commons

Tools are more useful when people can use them.

Part of the Workshop is experimenting with what it means to hold tools, equipment, knowledge, and eventually fabrication capacity as shared community infrastructure.

That requires more than a tool library.

It requires a culture of stewardship:

**Use things. Care for them. Learn how they work. Teach the next person. Leave the commons stronger than you found it.**

## How We Work

Relationships come before outcomes.

We move at the speed of trust.

We value curiosity over expertise, participation over credentials, and learning over pretending to already know.

We practice reciprocity: sometimes you teach, sometimes you learn, sometimes you repair the tool, sometimes you sweep the floor.

Decisions about the Workshop should increasingly be made by the people who participate in and care for it. We use practices such as sociocracy to experiment with shared governance, consent, responsibility, and stewardship.

## What This Could Become

We are starting small.

A bench. Some tools. Electronics. Computers. Sensors. Repairs. Building projects. People learning alongside one another.

But a workshop like this can become more than a room full of tools.

It can become a place where practical knowledge is preserved and passed on.

A place where someone discovers what they are capable of.

A place where an idea becomes a prototype.

A place where broken things become understandable again.

A place where neighbors learn to rely on one another.

A place where young people grow into skilled community members and eventually become the people teaching the next generation.

A small piece of the infrastructure needed for a resilient local community.

**Build something useful. Learn something real. Share what you know.**
